Her Names

Wuraola — Born Into Gold

Wura ni orúkọ rẹ — gold is your name, and every letter of it is the truth. Not the gold that shines only when life is easy — but the kind buried deep under earth and pressure, tested by fire, and still, still emerging pure and untouched.

Wuraola, ìwọ ni wura tòótọ́. Àwọn tí ó mọ ọ dára jùlọ ni wọn mọ iye rẹ. Orúkọ tí baba rẹ fún ọ kò ṣeéṣe jẹ́ àṣìṣe — wọn rí ọ kí o tó bí, wọn sì mọ pé ìwọ yóò jẹ́ olówó.

Those who know you best know your true worth. Your father's name for you was no mistake — they saw you before you arrived and knew you would be wealth itself.

Ajoke — ẹni a bá dúpẹ fún, ẹni a bá yọ̀. A soul to be praised, a heart to be celebrated. Your people named you with joy already in their mouths before you even cried your first cry. They were not wrong. Every person who has truly known you knows exactly why they called you Ajoke.

Ajoke temi — ẹni tí ọkàn rẹ mọ́, ẹni tí àánú rẹ jẹ gidi. Ìwà rẹ dára bí owó wúrà. Ẹ jẹ́ kí ayé mọ rẹ daradara bíi mo ṣe mọ ọ — kí wọn lè mọ ohun tí wọn ní nínú rẹ.

My Ajoke — whose heart is clean, whose kindness is real. Your character shines like refined gold. May the world come to know you the way I know you — so they understand what they have in you.

And your heritage — Omo Alkali Agba. Daughter of the Chief Judge. Ẹjẹ̀ ọlọlá ń ṣan nínú rẹ. The blood of dignity, of leadership, of honour flows through you. You were not born into ordinary soil — carry that truth with the quiet confidence it deserves.

Ọmọ Alkali Agba, ìwọ kò jẹ́ ọmọ aláìníye. Baba rẹ jẹ adájọ́ — ìdájọ́ ayé kò ní dá lòdì sí ọ. Ẹjẹ̀ ọlọlá ń ṣan nínú rẹ, àti ẹjẹ̀ yẹn kò gbọdọ̀ jẹ́ èké.

Daughter of the Chief Judge, you are not a child of nothing. Your father is a judge — life's verdict will not go against you. Noble blood flows in you, and that blood must never be in vain.

From My Heart

Àlá Mi Lójoojúmọ́ — What I Never Said Enough

Neemah, I want to be honest with you today — on your day — in the way I haven't always been able to put into words. There are people who pass through your life and leave traces. And then there are people who become part of how you see the world. You are the second kind for me.

Ìwọ ni àlá mi lójoojúmọ́ — kì í ṣe àlá tó jẹ́ ìrora, ṣùgbọ́n àlá tó jẹ́ òtítọ́. Bí ojú ọjọ́ ṣe ń mọ́ lójoojúmọ́ láìsí ẹni tó ń fipá mú, bẹ́ẹ̀ náà ni iwọ wà nínú ọkàn mi — pẹ̀lú àánú, pẹ̀lú ìrètí, pẹ̀lú ìfẹ́ tòótọ́.

You are my everyday dream — not a dream that hurts, but one that is simply true. The way the sun rises every morning without being forced to — that is how you live in my heart. With gentleness, with hope, with real love.

Life happened to us. I won't dress it up. Things shifted, roads curved, and what we had changed its shape. But Neemah — what never changed was you. You remained. Real. Consistent. Warm. The same person I saw in you from the very beginning.

That is rare. In a world where people show you who they are when things go wrong — you showed me who you are when things went quiet. And quiet loyalty is the loudest kind. I saw it. I still see it. I will always see it.

Ọ̀rẹ́ tòótọ́ ni ẹni tí ó wà nígbà àánú àti nígbà ayọ̀. Ìwọ jẹ́ ọ̀rẹ́ bẹ́ẹ̀. Àjọṣe wa le ti yí padà, ṣùgbọ́n ìfẹ́ tòótọ́ kò ní àpẹẹrẹ — ó ń tẹ̀ lé ọ̀nà tuntun láti wà. Tiwa wà. Tiwa ń bẹ. Tiwa kò lọ.

A true friend stands in both sorrow and joy. You are that friend. Our relationship may have changed shape, but true love has no fixed form — it finds new ways to remain. Ours remained. Ours exists. Ours never left.

I am with you, Neemah. Not because I have to be. Not because of what we were or what we could be. But because genuinely, honestly, quietly, firmly — you are someone I choose to root for every single day. Every. Single. Day.

Mo fẹ́ kí o mọ̀ pé ìwọ kò nìkan. Nígbà tí ohun gbogbo dàrú, nígbà tí ọ̀nà rẹ ṣoro, nígbà tí okàn rẹ rẹ̀ — mo wà. Kì í ṣe nítorí pé mo gbọdọ̀, ṣùgbọ́n nítorí pé ìwọ ní iye nínú ọkàn mi tí kò lè ṣàpèjúwe.

I want you to know that you are not alone. When everything falls apart, when your path gets hard, when your heart grows tired — I am here. Not because I must be, but because you hold a place in my heart that cannot be described.

Neemah's Closet Corner

Ìtàn Tí Kò Parí — The Story Isn't Over

I want to talk about Neemah's Closet Corner — because I refuse to let this birthday pass without saying something about it. I know what it cost you to build it. I know the energy, the faith, the late plans, the poured-out heart behind it. And I know what it felt like when things got heavy and you had to set it down.

Ìdúró kò jẹ́ ìkalọ́. Àwọn irúgbìn tí a bá gbìn kò gbọdọ̀ ṣòfo. Nígbà mìíràn, Ọlọrun gba wa là kúrò nínú ohun kan kí àkókò rẹ̀ tó tó — kì í ṣe nítorí pé àlá wa kéré, ṣùgbọ́n nítorí pé ilẹ̀ kò tíì tẹ́ fún àlá náà.

Rest is not defeat. Seeds that were planted must not be wasted. Sometimes God lifts us from something before its time is ready — not because our dream was small, but because the ground wasn't yet wide enough for it.

Neemah's Closet Corner was never a failure. It was a vision that arrived before the season was ready to hold it. And visions like that don't die — they wait. They grow quietly in the background while you rest, while you heal, while you gather yourself.

Insha'Allah, ọjọ́ rẹ̀ ń bọ̀. The day is coming when you will rise again — or something even more beautiful will be placed in your hands — and you will look back at this pause and say: "Ọlọrun mọ ohun tó ń ṣe." God knew what He was doing.
